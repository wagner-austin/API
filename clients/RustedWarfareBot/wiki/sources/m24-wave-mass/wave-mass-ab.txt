Nine matches, three seeds against each of three ladders, played in parallel on
four cloned game directories with the sample exchange locked to the tick at 75
frames. Everything except the final rung of the wave ladder is held fixed; the
early rungs govern the opening and are left at the shipped AI's.

The dip column is measured PER OPPONENT against that opponent's own running
peak. Measured against the leader instead -- which is what the first version of
this figure did -- every one of these runs reported a dip of about 700, because
the bot never damages whoever is currently ahead. That reading said the army
cost nobody anything, and it was an artefact of a max().

SUMMARY (three seeds per arm, identical code and economy, only the final rung
of the wave ladder differs)

  arm       army   worth    rival    ratio   worst dip   attack orders
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  mass 7    21.0   38,683   33,150   1.17        3,300             459
  mass 15   22.3   38,517   31,067   1.24        1,700             207
  mass 25   27.0   40,717   32,300   1.26        4,217             224

Economy is flat across the arms -- income 114-130/s, 12-14 extractors, 34-36
workers -- so the arms are cleanly isolated and what differs is how the units
were committed rather than how many were made. `reinforced` is 69-72 in every
one of the nine.

READING: massing more helps, mildly and consistently. A larger army survives
(21 -> 27) and order churn halves (459 -> 224), which is the same fact twice:
fewer, bigger commitments instead of a trickle. The worth ratio moves 1.17 ->
1.26, which is inside the run-to-run spread and is not on its own a result.

NOT SOLVED: nobody was eliminated in any of the nine, and the bot is ahead in
all of them. Our worth grew 11.6x from the opening position against the
strongest rival's 6.9x. The economy is won and never converted.
