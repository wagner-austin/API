Community research on Rusted Warfare strategy, and what it did and did not
settle
=========================================================================

Conducted 2026-07-26. Rusted Warfare is niche and most surviving material is
Steam Community guides of unstated version. Everything below is marked with its
confidence, and NOTHING here outranks the shipped INI files or the decompiled
1.15 (code 176, build #28) engine -- those are the pinned build we actually run.

Access note: steamcommunity.com returned HTTP 429 and rustedwarfare.fandom.com
returned HTTP 402 to direct fetches during this session, so several sources
below are quoted via search-engine extraction rather than a full page read.
That is weaker sourcing than reading the page and is flagged per item.


CONFIRMED INDEPENDENTLY -- economy: spread, do not upgrade
----------------------------------------------------------

Steam guide "A scientific approach to the economy system (kind of)" by Ionics,
posted 2020-05-14, game version unstated.
https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2095871975
(steamcommunity.com 429s on a bare fetch; appending &l=english succeeded)

  *** CONTENT-SET WARNING, added after reading the guide in full ***
  The guide tabulates "credit factories" at 25,000 / 47,500 / 70,000 credits
  generating 35 / 60 / 85 per tick. NO SUCH BUILDING EXISTS IN THIS BUILD. A
  grep of .game/assets/units/ finds `generation_resources` on exactly eight
  files -- the five extractors and the three fabricators -- and nothing in the
  tree is priced between 20,000 and 30,000. The guide is describing a mod or
  much older content.

  So it corroborates the METHOD (cost per unit of income, marginal, ignoring
  build time) and its extractor conclusion, and NONE of its numbers may be
  used. Everything numeric below and in measured-rates.txt comes from our own
  INI reads and live measurement.

  "Extractors are the clear winners because they are very cost effective. At
   the beginning, where most resource pools are still free, it is smarter to
   build more T1 extractors instead of fewer T2 or T3 extractors."

  "...fabricators become a viable option late-game after all resource pools
   have been occupied and upgraded."

This is the same conclusion measured-rates.txt reaches from the shipped INIs
and a live measurement, by an unrelated route. Our figures: a new T1 extractor
costs 87.5 credits per +1 generation, an upgrade costs 350-800, a fabricator
1100. Confidence: HIGH, two independent sources agreeing.

The guide's own caveat is worth carrying: it "focuses primarily on the cost per
income ratio and ignores build times". Ours ignores builder travel time, which
is the same blind spot -- a distant pool costs a walk that an in-base upgrade
does not.

Its fabricator conclusions DO hold for this build, which is a point in favour of
its reasoning even though its unit table is from elsewhere. The guide says T2
fabricators beat both T1 and T3, and that adding a new T2 beats upgrading an
existing one to T3. Our INI arithmetic, cost per +1 generation:

  upgrade fabricatorT1 -> T2    4400 / +5   =  880   <- cheapest
  new fabricatorT2              6600 /  7   =  943
  new fabricatorT3             14100 / 14   = 1007
  upgrade fabricatorT2 -> T3    7500 / +7   = 1071
  new fabricatorT1              2200 /  2   = 1100   <- worst

Both claims check out: 943 beats 1100 and 1007, and 943 beats 1071. All of it
is academic while a pool is free, though -- a new extractor is 87.5.


THE OPPONENTS ARE ON MEDIUM, AND MEDIUM DOES NOT CHEAT
-------------------------------------------------------

Community claim, repeated across several Steam discussion threads and via
search extraction (not read in full):

  "The normal AI doesn't cheat, but hard to impossible AIs get a multiplier
   boost to their income... easy AIs have an income multiplier handicap."
  "on higher difficulties the AI's behaviour is also different, it attacks with
   bigger groups of units and uses experimentals."
  "stats of individual units are never affected by difficulty changes."

  https://steamcommunity.com/app/647960/discussions/0/1678063648167735993/
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/647960/discussions/0/1628538707069444336/

Verified locally, which matters more:

  .game/assets/translations/Strings.properties lines 55-61 give the enum --
    -2 Very Easy, -1 Easy, 0 Medium, 1 Hard, 2 Very Hard, 3 Impossible

  .game/preferences.ini line 2 -- aiDifficulty:0

  .decompiled/com/corrodinggames/rts/gameFramework/j/ah.java -- the room
    settings object carries `incomeMultiplier` (field h) defaulting to 1.0f
    alongside startingCredits, aiDifficulty, startingUnits and randomSeed.

So our sandbox skirmishes run against five **Medium** opponents at an income
multiplier of 1.0. There is no handicap to blame. Every run this bot has lost
was lost on equal income against the baseline difficulty.

NOT verified: no per-difficulty income multiplier table was found anywhere in
the decompiled tree. Greps for handicap/multiplier/difficulty-scaled resource
terms returned only `addResourcesScaledByAIHandicaps`, a mod-facing INI key on
unit actions, not a global income model. The community claim may be true and
implemented somewhere this search missed, may be version-drifted, or may be
folk knowledge. It does not affect us either way at difficulty 0.


UNCONFIRMED -- opening and attack timing
-----------------------------------------

Several guides describe an aggressive opening. Via search extraction only:

  "If there's a land connection, rush your tanks to approximately midway, then
   frantically try to get a foothold on the middle of the map with some
   turrets, claim the extractors, and build up advanced units at your base."

  "First find your Extractor Mines and place an Extractor on top of it. With
   your remaining Combat Engineers, build 2 Anti Airs and 3 ground turrets."

  https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1858207046
  https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1449760671

Confidence: LOW. No version stated, written for human multiplayer, and the
"combat engineers" opening does not match our sandbox start (one Builder, one
Command Center). Contest-the-middle advice is about map control on specific
maps, and our bot plays one fixed map.

Worth noting only because it agrees with the shipped AI's own configuration on
static defence, which we can read directly and therefore trust more: turrets at
buildPriority ~0.2 with maxEachBase 3-5, anti-air included. Our bot builds
zero of either.


UNCONFIRMED -- unit composition
--------------------------------

  "Rusted Warfare has a rock-paper-scissors-like system... Sturdy tanks can
   soak up damage but are vulnerable to anti-armor units, and powerful
   artillery can decimate enemy bases from afar but needs protection."
  "mass T1 Land Units... then build one or two Mobile Turrets buffered by
   smaller units, as the AOE damage, range, and firing rate of the mobile
   turrets will destroy large groups of smaller units."

  https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1858207046

Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM, and untested here. Our bot builds only c_tank. The
claim that a small number of AOE units breaks up massed cheap units is
plausible and testable against our own A/B harness, which is the right way to
settle it rather than taking a guide's word.


QUESTIONS THE RESEARCH DID NOT ANSWER
--------------------------------------

1. No community build order exists for our actual situation -- a scripted bot,
   one fixed map, five Medium AI opponents, sandbox mode. Everything found is
   human-multiplayer advice.
2. No numeric attack-timing benchmark. Nothing says "attack at N minutes" or
   "expand until N extractors".
3. Whether static defence pays for itself in skirmish was not settled. Guides
   recommend it; the shipped AI builds it; neither is evidence about our bot,
   which loses field battles rather than base assaults.
4. No per-difficulty income multiplier values, as above.
5. Nothing on tech timing that distinguishes T1-only play from teching, at any
   difficulty.

The honest summary: the web confirmed the one thing we had already proven from
primary sources, and supplied nothing decisive about anything else. The
remaining questions are all answerable with the seeded A/B harness we already
have, which is a better instrument for them than a ten-year-old forum post.
