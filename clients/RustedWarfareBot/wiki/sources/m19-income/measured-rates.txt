What an extractor is worth, measured live and cross-checked against the
engine's own unit definitions
===========================================================================

Two independent sources, agreeing to within 0.1%.


1. MEASURED (make income INCOME_STAGES=4 INCOME_IDLE=200)
---------------------------------------------------------

One match. The bot builds one extractor at a time and stands completely still
for 200 samples between each, ordering nothing, so every window's credit slope
is income and nothing else. Readings archived beside this file as
income-windows.ndjson (1000 of them).

  window  extractors  readings   span_ms   gained   credits/s
       0           0       200     49904     1345       26.95
       1           1       200     49897     1946       39.00
       2           2       200     49821     2545       51.08
       3           3       200     49929     3150       63.09
       4           4       200     49822     3737       75.01

  per extractor  12.01 credits/s
  payback        58.3s at 700 credits

The successive differences are +12.05, +12.08, +12.01, +11.92. Linear to the
limit of the measurement, which is what a fixed per-unit generation rate should
look like and is the main reason to trust it.

The 26.95/s at zero extractors is the Command Center. It is a built-in engine
unit rather than a shipped .ini, so its rate is not readable as text -- but
26.95 / 1.5 = 17.97, i.e. a generation value of 18 under the conversion derived
below.


2. THE ENGINE'S OWN DEFINITIONS (.game/assets/units/)
------------------------------------------------------

Extractors and fabricators are plain INI files shipped with the game, not
obfuscated bytecode. The generation figure is stated outright:

  extractor/extractor.ini              price:   700   generation_resources: credits=8
  extractor/extractorT2.ini            price:  2100   generation_resources: credits=12
  extractor/extractorT3.ini            price:  6100   generation_resources: credits=20
  extractor/extractorT3_reinforced.ini price: 10100   generation_resources: credits=20
  extractor/extractorT3_overclocked.ini price:14100   generation_resources: credits=30
  fabricator/fabricatorT1.ini          price:  2200   generation_resources: credits=2
  fabricator/fabricatorT2.ini          price:  6600   generation_resources: credits=7
  fabricator/fabricatorT3.ini          price: 14100   generation_resources: credits=14

The conversion to credits per second follows from the measurement: 8 generation
units measured 12.01 credits/s, so

  credits per second = generation_resources x 1.5

That 1.5 is not arbitrary. The parser defaults `generation_delay` to 40 ticks
and scales by `40.0f / generation_delay`
(.decompiled/com/corrodinggames/rts/game/units/custom/ag.java:1500-1506), so a
simulation ticking 60 times a second yields exactly 60/40 = 1.5. The 60 is
inferred from that arithmetic rather than read directly, but every measured
rate in the table above fits it.


3. THE DECISION THIS SETTLES: SPREAD, DO NOT UPGRADE
-----------------------------------------------------

Marginal cost per unit of income -- what each option costs to buy one more
credit of generation:

  action                        cost   +generation   cost per +1   payback
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  build a new extractorT1        700           +8          87.5      58.3s
  upgrade T1 -> T2              1400           +4         350.0     233.3s
  upgrade T2 -> T3              4000           +8         500.0     333.3s
  upgrade T3 -> overclocked     8000          +10         800.0     533.3s
  build a new fabricatorT1      2200           +2        1100.0     733.3s

A new T1 extractor is FOUR TIMES more credit-efficient than upgrading one, and
TWELVE AND A HALF TIMES more efficient than a fabricator.

This is not a reading imposed on the data -- it is how the game's own authors
annotated these files. Their comments, left in the shipped INIs:

  extractor.ini                  "#price per credit: $87"
  extractorT3.ini (overclocked)  "#price per credit: $800 (+10)"
  fabricatorT1.ini               "#price per credit: $1100"

$87.50, $800 and $1100 are exactly the marginal figures in the table.

So: take every free pool before upgrading anything, and never build a
fabricator while a pool is free. Fabricators and upgrades are what you buy when
the map runs out of pools -- the fabricator does not need one, which is the
whole of its value.

Caveat worth keeping: the reinforced T3 buys no income at all (20 -> 20) for
3000 credits. It buys hit points, 2000 -> 4700. It is a survivability purchase
and does not belong in this table's logic.


4. WHAT THE SHIPPED AI DOES
----------------------------

From extractor_common.ini, the [ai] block the opponents are driven by:

  buildPriority: 0.4
  recommendedInEachBasePriorityIfUnmet: 0.55
  recommendedInEachBaseNum: 2
  noneInBaseExtraPriority: 0.43
  maxEachBase: 99

maxEachBase is 99. The opponents are not aiming at a fixed number of
extractors; they take what they can reach. A bot capped at three was not
playing a different strategy, it was playing no economic strategy at all.


5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE RUN LENGTH WE ACTUALLY PLAY
-------------------------------------------------------

200 samples spanned 49.9 seconds, so a sample is about 0.25s and the standard
1500-sample fight phase is roughly 375 seconds.

An extractor bought at the start of that phase pays for itself in 58 seconds
and then returns 12 credits/s for the remaining ~317, netting about 3,800
credits -- eleven more tanks at 350 each. Expansion is not a marginal call at
this time scale; it is the dominant one.
