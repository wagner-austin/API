Video Game / Rusted Warfare
Following

Go To
  • VideoGame
  • Create Subpage

Rusted Warfare is a Multiplayer Real-Time Strategy game released in 2017 by Corroding Games. Seeking to emulate the nostalgia of old Real-Time Strategy games, it utilises assets from the now-canceled Hard Vacuum. 

To allow for thousands of units to be shown on screen with minimal performance impacts, the game uses modern optimisation and advanced multi-core threading techniques to be able to simulate hundreds of units at once without straining game performance to a significant degree. The game features offline and online gamemodes, with multiple other bonus modes like missions, an endless survival mode and a challenge mode, all of which can be played offline.

Rusted Warfare contains examples of:

  • Anti-Air:
    • You can build Anti-Air turrets, which can then be upgraded to either a T2 or T3 variant with general bonuses across the line. Alternatively, the player can opt to upgrade a T1 Anti-Air turret into a flak turret.
    • Many units that can be produced are specialised to destroy air targets, such as Interceptors or Anti-Air Mechs which can attack flying units but lack any sort of attack for grounded units.
  • Airborne Aircraft Carrier: The Flying Fortress is able to carry twelve units at a time and has a built-in fabricator. The Flying Fortress both can target other air or ground units, depending on if it is currently flying.
  • Construct Additional Pylons: In order to expand and fight the opposing teams, the player is required to create factories and defenses. Multiple variants of the factory exist specialised for the production of one kind of unit, and the game provides a wealthy amount of turrets to defend your factories with.
  • Defog of War: Every unit and building in the game constantly reveal their surroundings as long as they remain intact. Certain buildings like the T2 Artillery or units such as the Scout are capable of seeing much further through the fog, stat-wise. 
  • Defenseless Transports: Dropships and Landing Craft, respectively, are able to carry up to four units across the map. Accordingly, they lack any offensive capabilities.
  • Drop Ship: The player can construct a 'dropship', which can carry up to 4 units at a time.
  • Easy Logistics: Units and buildings do not require any form of upkeep and resource nodes will start adding up to your income rate as soon as their respective extractors are built with no need to process them into other materials.
  • ​Endless Game: The 'Survival' gamemode pits the player against increasingly large numbers of enemy units who will attempt to destroy your HQ. The game ends once all of your buildings are destroyed.
  • Equipment Upgrade: On average, every building in the game is capable of being upgraded to a T2 variant, and rarely, a T3 variant. Upgrading often provides better stats and unique abilities at a steeper cost. Upgraded factories are capable of producing a wider variety of units.
  • Epic Ship-on-Ship Action: Certain naval units are capable of fighting with eachother on ocean tiles, engaging in naval battles.
  • Fog of War: Unexplored parts of the map are obscured in a near pitch black darkness. Any tiles explored by the player previously will be rendered with a slightly less opaque black fog and will show any enemy building positions as they were last seen by the player. Any player building or unit will remove the fog completely within their weapon radius.
  • Geo Effects: Certain units are unable to traverse certain terrain. Most ground units cannot pass over cliffs, while every ground unit cannot pass over lava. Likewise, aerial units can bypass these limitations completely.
  • Hard-Coded Hostility: The game lacks any traditional diplomacy that can be used to negotiate with the AI. Unless manually swapped onto your same team outside of a game, the AI will always be coded to expand and fight your bases regardless of the economic disparity.
  • Hover Tank: The player can construct the 'Hovertank', which is a fairly weak unit. The player can build an upgraded 'Shielded Hovertank' if they have a T2 factory.
  • Non-Entity General: You command your units through a top-down view of the map. You are given no visible avatar or identity - beyond being the ambigious leader or general of your team.
  • Military Mashup Machine: The Experimental Spider can be effectively described as a mix of nearly every single unit in the game. Posessing shielding, laser interceptors, an anti-air gun and two main turrets, the Experimental Spider is capable of producing a wide range of units, building upgraded factories off the bat,  and can generally raise hell in any battle it's deployed to.
  • Not Playing Fair With Resources: Depending on the difficulty that is set to each AI, the credit generation rate can be higher then the player through increasingly higher multipliers. 
  • Nuclear Nullifier: The player can construct the Nuke Interceptor, which  are capable of storing twelve anti-missiles at a time, and will destroy any deployed nuclear weapons approaching them. They will only target a nuclear missile that has been set to detonate in any area that is within the radius of an interceptor.
  • No Blood for Phlebotinum: Every game of Rusted Warfare comes down to a fight between the player and the enemy for control over the various resource nodes spread across the map, which is central for expanding and upkeeping your war effort.
  • Purposely Overpowered: The Experimentals are essentially the final, ultimate versions of each given archtype of unit in the game. Expensive to build, and requiring a fairly expensive factory to do so, a hallmark of late-game battles are multiple Experimentals participating in battles. Often carrying superior firepower and health, the only hard counter to an Experimental is either overwhelming them with larger numbers, or having sufficiently upgraded defenses.
  • Spider Tank: Downplayed with the Experimental Spider, which has 6 legs. Played straight with the Modular Spider, which has 8 legs. Both of them are the most powerful units in the game, and some of the most expensive as well.
  • Separate, but Identical: The AI is able to build just about anything that the player can, including modded content. The only distinct difference visually is the accented colours of each building and unit, which change accordingly to the team they belong to. However, while the AI is capable of building nuclear silos, they cannot use them against the player.
  • Slap-on-the-Wrist Nuke: A single nuclear missile is capable of destroying even an Experimental Spider, but has a relatively small range of destruction.
  • Tactical Superweapon Unit: All the experimental units often sport a larger healthpool and have many more offensive and defensive capabilities compared to the standard units that can be produced by normal factories. The Experimental Spider can be comparable to an entire walking base, with the capabilities to not only build but also support other units with a built-in fabricator.
  • Tank Goodness: Many of the units the player can construct are tanks. There is also an Experimental Tank which is the most powerful type of tank in the game that the player can use.
  • Worker Unit: The player will be required to produce 'Builder' units to make factories and buildings. Builders are one of the cheapest units in the game and do not have any means of offense.
  • You Require More Vespene Gas: The player will be required to capture resource nodes and build Fabricators to accumulate a steady flow of Credits, which are required to produce new units and construct new buildings.
  • You Nuke 'Em: If the player not only has the money to build a nuclear silo, but to also build a nuclear bomb itself, the player now has a largely reliable way to take out enemy bases without losing units. The only exception is, of course, is if the AI was able to build interceptors in time.
  • Zerg Rush: A viable tactic in the game is to send swarms of cheap units (most often Light Gun Ships) to overwhelm and drain the energy of laser defenses. This leaves buildings vulnerable to hard-hitting units, who can now take the risk to lead an offensive.
