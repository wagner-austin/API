Production timing, measured against a live headless sandbox
===========================================================

Method: connect over the agent channel, pick the Command Center's option for
the named type from the sample's own option list, send one produce order, then
count samples until a unit appears in the owned roster that was not there
before. Probe source kept out of the repo; the procedure is the record.

Game: Rusted Warfare 1.15 (code 176, build #28), -nodisplay -nosound -sandbox.
Producer: unit 213, commandCenter, the only producer a fresh sandbox owns.

  HEADER produce scout at unit 213 frame=2712 credits=4243 owned=3
    step 20 credits=3678
    step 40 credits=3813
  RESULT produce_samples=45 appeared=['scout'] frames=3378 credits=3849

  HEADER produce builder at unit 213 frame=9993 credits=4200 owned=4
    step 20 credits=3835
  RESULT produce_samples=34 appeared=['builder'] frames=2553 credits=3929

Prices from the archived -printunits catalogue: builder $500, scout $700.

  builder   500 / 34 = 14.7 credits per sample
  scout     700 / 45 = 15.6 credits per sample

Linear in price within 6% across the two, which is what makes a produce window
derivable rather than guessed. The rate belongs to the producing building's own
build speed; both figures here are the Command Center's.

Second finding, and the one that rules out the obvious alternative: credits do
NOT fall monotonically while producing. The scout run reads 4243 -> 3678 ->
3813 -> 3849 -- down at first, then RISING through the rest of production as
income outpaced the drain. So "credits decreasing" cannot serve as an
in-flight signal the way builder movement does for travel. At the time this
left only a price-derived window; the third run below supersedes it.


Third run, with the entity queue depth now carried in the world stream
=====================================================================

  HEADER produce scout at unit 213 frame=1675 credits=4153 owned=3
    step 10 credits=3520 queued={213: 1, 214: 0, 217: 0}
    step 20 credits=3588 queued={213: 1, 214: 0, 217: 0}
    step 30 credits=3655 queued={213: 1, 214: 0, 217: 0}
    step 40 credits=3723 queued={213: 1, 214: 0, 217: 0}
  RESULT produce_samples=45 appeared=['scout'] frames=3377 credits=3754

The producing building reports queued=1 for the whole of production and zero on
the sample the unit appears, and the 45-sample timing reproduces exactly. That
is a real in-flight signal, the production counterpart of builder movement, and
it retires the price-derived window this file was originally measured for: no
rate constant, no safety factor, and no cap on what the bot can afford to make.

Note the credits column again -- 3520, 3588, 3655, 3723, rising monotonically
through a production the queue reports as active. Whatever else is true, the
balance is not a progress signal.
