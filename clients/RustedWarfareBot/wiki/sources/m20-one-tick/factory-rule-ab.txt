Throughput rule, four shapes, one seed (12345), 1500 samples each.

Question: the bot ends every match holding five figures of unspent credits.
Is it throughput-bound -- too few factories to convert income into units?

  rule                              factories  extractors  income  army value  credits left
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A  "every producer busy"                  0          10    98/s        6450        18576
  B  A, restricted to wanted types          1           9    90/s        4000        16412
  C  surplus after all claims               7           7    74/s        3300        10270
  D  B and C together (shipped)             1           9    90/s        5400        16442

A never fired at all: a producer was any unit offering a non-placed action,
which the Command Center always does because it can make a Builder, and it is
idle almost permanently. The answer was "no" on every observation of every
match.

B fired once. A factory is busy for the whole of a build and idle for the single
tick in which it finishes -- which is the tick the rule is evaluated on, because
that is when production had capacity to fill.

C fired freely and was the worst of the four. The bank drained, and the army
shrank with it.

The mechanism is not run-to-run variance. Income is the low-variance figure --
it is a deterministic function of extractor count -- and it falls monotonically
as factories rise: 98, 90, 74. There is one builder, and every factory it places
is an extractor it does not. Income gates production, so buying capacity with
the credits that would have bought income trades the thing that was working for
the thing that was idle.

D is the conjunction, and it is deliberately conservative: queues genuinely full
AND money genuinely spare.

What this does NOT establish: why the credits accumulate in the first place.
Twenty-eight reinforcements over 1500 samples is far below what even two
factories can produce, so something other than factory count is throttling the
spend. The unit cap is the first candidate -- the agent already reads the
engine's own availability predicate, which accounts for it -- and the per-sample
trace (rw_bot.policy.trace) is the instrument that would settle it. Adding
factories is not the experiment; it was the guess, and it was wrong.
